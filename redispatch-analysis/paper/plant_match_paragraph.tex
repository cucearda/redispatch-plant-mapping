As a first step, we filter out all events for which the reason for the
redistribution (\texttt{GRUND\_DER\_MASSNAHME}) was specified as something other
than ``load induced'', thereby excluding test runs (\emph{Probefahrten}) and
minor measure types that we do not consider in our analysis. In the next step,
all events that are marked as Counter Trading or where the affected plant is
``Exchange'' (\emph{B\"orse}) are also eliminated. Counter trading and
exchange-based measures relieve congestion indirectly, by buying and selling
additional electricity on the market, rather than directly instructing
individual plants to change their output. We remove the data because there is no
way to determine where the bottleneck was that required corrective action.

For all remaining events, the affected plant entry is matched to a physical
plant location via a comprehensive pipeline. The pipeline's steps are outlined
in Algorithm~\ref{alg:plant-matcher}. Before any lookup, the raw plant string is
normalised and cleaned: mojibake is repaired, umlauts are ASCII-folded, and
transmission-system-operator codes, plant-type abbreviations, block and turbine
unit codes, and other descriptor noise are stripped, leaving the significant
place tokens. Each plant is also assigned an energy class
(\emph{konventionell}, \emph{erneuerbar}, or \emph{sonstiges}) derived from its
primary energy carrier, which is used to reject candidates whose fuel type
contradicts the plant.

The pipeline iterates through 7 different steps for every location, stopping as
soon as a candidate reaches a confidence above $0.85$. The steps are, in order:
(0)~an aggregation filter that intercepts grid-area, cluster, and pool entries,
resolving them only to a coarse regional, state, or DSO-area centroid, and
discarding purely market-based constructs (e.g. \emph{B\"orse}, minimum reserve,
EIC codes) that have no meaningful coordinate; (1)~a direct match against the
OPSD database, first exact and then fuzzy; (2)~a direct match against the PyPSA
plant list, again exact and then fuzzy; (3)~for conventional and
\emph{sonstiges} plants, a German Wikipedia API search using the cleaned name
plus a plant-type suffix (e.g.\ ``Kraftwerk''), with a DuckDuckGo search as a
fallback; (4)~for the remaining plants, a place-name extraction resolved against
the German pgeocode gazetteer, complemented by state- and region-centroid
lookups; (5)~for renewables not yet located, a Wikipedia search (with
DuckDuckGo fallback) using ``Windpark''/``Solarpark'' suffixes; and (6)~a
last-resort Nominatim forward geocode when nothing else matched. Every Wikipedia
and DuckDuckGo candidate must pass a set of strict gates (title blocklist, token
overlap, presence of coordinates within the German--Luxembourg--Austrian bounding
box, and no energy-type contradiction) before it is accepted. Each step records
its outcome independently, and the final coordinate is chosen as the
highest-confidence candidate across all steps.

The confidence score calculation can be seen in Table~\ref{tab:confidence}. The
reasoning behind the confidence scoring is as following: The highest confidence
is assigned to direct matches to the OPSD database, which is a list of plants
provided by Netztransparenz directly. The second highest confidence is assigned
to direct matches to the PyPSA plant list, a list maintained by the Technical
University of Berlin. After this, both OPSD and PyPSA are fuzzy matched against
the string. For this, they get assigned a base confidence multiplied with a
fuzzy match coefficient between 0 and 1, obtained as the Ratcliff--Obershelp
similarity ratio between the cleaned plant name and the candidate string (the
\texttt{difflib.SequenceMatcher} implementation), with a fuzzy match only being
considered above a cutoff of $0.88$ for OPSD and $0.90$ for PyPSA. The remaining,
web- and gazetteer-based sources are assigned successively lower base
confidences, reflecting that they identify a plausible location rather than a
verified plant entry: a Wikipedia match scores $0.85$, a DuckDuckGo-mediated
Wikipedia match $0.70$, an OPSD city-name match $0.55$, and a gazetteer
place-name hit $0.50$, while coarse state-, region-, and DSO-area centroids
receive the lowest scores. Two adjustments are applied on top of the base score:
a candidate whose energy class contradicts the plant's primary energy carrier is
set to zero, and a PyPSA match that is ambiguous because several equally named
plants share the string is penalised in proportion to the number of competing
